Back in 2024, I realized that my websites would work way better if I rewrote them in Astro. This is
the culmination of almost 9 months of working with Astro.

## Accomplishments

- Rebuilt and rewrote websites using a unified template
- Added thematic branding

## Skills

- Extensive use of CSS, HTML, and Javascript
- Use of Cloudflare Pages + Workers to deploy static pages
- Wrote simple Astro components to standardize design
- Used TailwindCSS to stylize a website

## Background

Back in January of 2025, I was finishing up rewriting all my websites in Astro in time for college application season.
Most college applications have a field for me to input a personal portfolio, so I took advantage of that.

However, it was clear that my skills with this were lacking. I barely knew how to code in Javascript, I didn't know
much HTML, and if you asked me to do something in Javascript, it probably would've ended up with me turning to
StackOverflow.

However, things have changed. I've been coding even more since then, and I'm taking another stab at rewriting my websites
now that I don't have to worry about content.

## The Process

The main thing I wanted to do with this project was to create reusable components that would fit in place with any
other website I created. That's why I stuck with the template I used for my music website - it's simplistic, performant,
and I already wrote a few "components" that would easily scale to anything I threw at it. (It turns out that I wasn't
the worst at HTML and CSS!)

From there, it was a matter of simply copying over what I'd done and changing up everything to make it work.

## The Colors

One thing I didn't want to do with a unified design language was to confuse people over which website they were visiting.

That's where the subtle use of colors came in. I chose purple for my music website because that's what I've long
associated my music profile with, and then I had a few other colors to choose from.

For Curtain Lorimer, I chose an emerald green-based design, reminiscent of older colors I used in the past.

For my portfolio, I stuck with a light shade of blue, reminiscent of both my room's walls and the thing that
got me started on thinking forward with design - Material You, from the team at Google.

## The Design

As previously stated, I had already written some components that would easily map to a new website. I made it a priority to create more components that would scale to any other venture I created - that way, it'd be simple for me to update and upgrade anything as necessary.

I first decided to make everything more vivid - including the buttons, which I changed to be full-width. Bolder colors need to be complemented by other changes, so I swapped out the old, minimalistic design for one which filled the screen with content (much like a bento box).

The result was something I thought looked clean enough for the time being. I'll revise my websites with various little tweaks (and I'll probably end up revamping my music website for every release, anyways).

## The Rollout

I proceeded to roll everything out on Cloudflare Pages, something that I'd been using for its free services and for its extensive network. I'm in the process of transfering everything over to Cloudflare Workers (as Pages is going defunct in a few years).
